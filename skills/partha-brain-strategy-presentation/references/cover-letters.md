# McKinsey (and MBB) Cover Letter Rules

Source: Hacking the Case Interview, "McKinsey Cover Letter: Step-By-Step Guide (2026)." This skill converts that guide into an enforceable rule set for drafting or critiquing a cover letter. Apply every rule below as a checklist — do not skip the quantification and specificity rules even if the user's raw material is sparse; flag gaps instead of writing around them with vague language.

## 0. First move

Before drafting, get (or infer from what's given):
1. **Candidate type** — Undergraduate/summer, MBA/post-grad, or Experienced hire/career changer. This changes which stories to pull and how much "why consulting" justification is needed (see Section 5).
2. **Target firm** — McKinsey, BCG, or Bain (or another firm). Section 2 has the comparison table; do not reuse a McKinsey letter unedited for another firm.
3. **Target role** — Business Analyst / Associate (or Summer variants). Get this exact — wrong title is an instant red flag.
4. **Raw material** — 3+ candidate accomplishments/stories the user can draw on, ideally already with numbers. If they don't have numbers, ask for them before writing rather than inventing figures.

If the user hasn't given enough raw material to fill three body paragraphs with quantified stories, say so directly and ask for the missing accomplishments — don't pad with generic claims to hit word count.

## 1. Non-negotiable format specs

- **Length: 250–400 words, one page, no exceptions.** Longer signals poor communication — the opposite of what's being tested.
- **Structure: exactly 5 sections** — contact info, salutation, opening paragraph, 3 body paragraphs, concluding paragraph.
- **Font:** Times New Roman, Calibri, or Cambria, 10–12pt body text (name at top: 14–18pt, bold).
- **Margins:** 1 inch, all sides. **Alignment:** left (not justified). **Line spacing:** 1.15, with a blank line between paragraphs.
- **Header:** match the resume's header format for visual consistency.
- **Salutation:** never "To Whom It May Concern." Address the named recruiter if known; otherwise "Dear members of the [Firm] Recruiting Team,"

## 2. What each firm is actually testing (do not cross-apply)

| | McKinsey | BCG | Bain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Required? | Optional at most offices | Required for most applications | Optional but recommended |
| Primary purpose | Explain your story and motivation | Demonstrate intellectual curiosity | Show cultural fit and collaboration |
| Core qualities tested | Personal impact, problem solving, leadership | Analytical skills, creativity, drive | Passion, teamwork, results orientation |
| Ideal length | 250–400 words | 300–400 words | 250–350 words |

**Sanity check before sending:** swap the firm name in the letter for a competitor's. If the letter still reads fine unchanged, it's too generic — rewrite until it only makes sense for the target firm (specific program, publication, practice area, or event referenced).

## 3. McKinsey's four core qualities

Every letter should hit 2–3 of these, each backed by a specific, numbered example — never asserted as an adjective alone.

| Quality | What McKinsey wants to see | Weak signal (avoid) | Strong signal (use) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal Impact | Your actions changed outcomes for a team/project/org | "I made a big difference" | "Led a pricing analysis that increased client revenue by $12M annually" |
| Entrepreneurial Drive | Initiative taken without being asked | "I'm self-motivated" | "Launched a student consulting club from scratch, growing it to 80 members in one year" |
| Leadership | Inspired, organized, and guided a team to results | "I'm a strong leader" | "Managed a cross-functional team of 6 to deliver a market entry strategy in 3 weeks" |
| Problem Solving | Structured, analytical approach to complex challenges | "I'm analytical" | "Built a customer segmentation model using 500K data points that identified $8M in savings" |

**Rule: every claim needs a receipt.** If a sentence contains an adjective about the candidate ("strong," "passionate," "analytical") with no example attached in the same paragraph, rewrite or cut it.

## 4. Section-by-section rules

### 4a. Contact block
Full name (bold, 14–18pt), email, phone, mailing address. Nothing else.

### 4b. Opening paragraph — exactly 2 sentences
- **Sentence 1:** One-sentence summary of background/expertise/years of experience. This is the "if I had 10 seconds, what's my whole career" sentence.
- **Sentence 2:** Names the exact role (correct title — Business Analyst / Associate / Summer BA / Summer Associate) and previews the **three reasons** the candidate is a fit. Each of these three reasons becomes one body paragraph — decide the three reasons first, then write this sentence, then write the paragraphs, in that order.
- Most recruiters read only this paragraph and skim the rest — it carries more weight than its length suggests. Do not bury the role title or the three reasons anywhere else.

*Template:* "I am a [role/field] professional with [X] years of experience [doing what, at what scale/companies]. I believe my [reason 1], [reason 2], and [reason 3] make me a great fit for the [exact role title] at [Firm]."

### 4c. Body paragraphs — exactly 3, one per reason from the opening
Each paragraph:
1. **Opens with a bolded one-sentence summary** containing a metric. Many recruiters read only this first sentence of each paragraph — it must stand alone.
2. **Follows with 2–4 sentences**: situation → your specific action → quantified result. No filler, no throat-clearing.
3. **Maps to exactly one of the four core qualities** (Section 3) — don't let two paragraphs argue the same quality.

*Template:* "**My [X] enabled me to [headline result].** [1 sentence: situation/context]. [1–2 sentences: specific actions taken]. [1 sentence: quantified outcome, ideally $ / % / time / scale]."

**Weak vs. strong — hold every paragraph to this bar:**
- Weak: "I am a strong leader who has led teams to success in multiple projects. I enjoy working with people and believe my leadership skills would be valuable at McKinsey." — no numbers, no specific project, interchangeable with any other candidate.
- Strong: "I led a 6-person cross-functional team to deliver a go-to-market strategy for a $40M product launch, completing the project 2 weeks ahead of schedule and exceeding first-quarter sales targets by 18%." — scale, timeline, and measurable result all present.

### 4d. Concluding paragraph — exactly 2 sentences
- **Sentence 1:** Restate the three reasons from the opening (callback, not new material).
- **Sentence 2:** Ask for the interview directly.

*Template:* "Due to my [reason 1], [reason 2], and [reason 3], I believe I have the qualities to become a successful [Firm] consultant. I would love the opportunity to further discuss my candidacy and fit in an interview."

This repetition (state it, prove it, restate it) is intentional — it's what makes the letter legible on a skim.

## 5. Tailoring by candidate type

- **Undergraduate / Summer BA:** Limited professional experience is expected — pull from extracurricular leadership, academic research, and internships. Emphasize quantifiable results from clubs, competitions, or part-time work; student-org leadership is a strong, well-precedented story choice at this level.
- **MBA / Post-graduate:** Focus on pre-MBA professional accomplishments. Must explicitly connect past career to consulting — e.g., finance background → analytical rigor; operations background → process-improvement experience. The "why consulting now" thread should run through the letter, not just the opening.
- **Experienced hire / Career changer:** Highest bar — must justify the career switch. Lead with the most impressive professional accomplishment and explicitly connect it to consulting work. Surface any industry expertise the firm values (healthcare, tech, financial services) by name. Use the letter to preempt the obvious question a recruiter will have about the switch.

## 6. Handling red flags (only if applicable)

If the candidate has a low GPA, an employment gap, or is applying to a city with no obvious connection to them, address it in 1–2 sentences and pivot to strength — don't leave it for the recruiter to wonder about, and don't over-explain:
- Low GPA → mention grade trend upward, or additional qualifications proving capability.
- Employment gap → what productive thing filled it (courses, volunteering, a venture).
- Unusual office choice → genuine connection to the city or a specific long-term reason for that geography.

## 7. Personalization requirements

- Never use generic consulting-firm language ("help clients solve their toughest problems") — it fits every firm and proves nothing.
- Reference something specific to the target firm: a named program, a McKinsey Global Institute publication, a practice area, or an event/conversation.
- Networking mentions are allowed but capped at **one sentence**, naming the person and what was actually discussed — never a list of names. The point is to show the conversation changed something, not that networking happened.
- Address "why consulting" (not just "why this firm") explicitly, especially for career changers — show awareness of what the job actually involves (long hours, travel, constant context-switching) and that it's wanted, not just tolerated.

## 8. Mistake checklist — run every draft through this before finalizing

- [ ] Is this a narrative, or does it just re-list resume bullet points? (If it repeats the resume, cut and replace with a story the resume can't tell.)
- [ ] Does every quality claim have a specific, quantified example attached?
- [ ] Is it 250–400 words and one page?
- [ ] Would this letter still make sense if you swapped in a competitor firm's name? (If yes, it's too generic — fix per Section 2.)
- [ ] Any typos or grammar errors? (Treat this letter as a writing sample — proofread multiple times.)
- [ ] Are networking mentions substantive (what was learned) rather than name-dropping?
- [ ] Does the exact role title match what the candidate is applying for?
- [ ] Do the three reasons in the opening match the three body paragraph topics exactly, in the same order?

## 9. Quick-reference numbers

- One-page limit, 250–400 words total (BCG: 300–400; Bain: 250–350).
- Opening paragraph: 2 sentences. Concluding paragraph: 2 sentences.
- Exactly 3 body paragraphs, each opening with a bolded, metric-bearing summary sentence.
- Cover letter moves borderline candidates into the interview pile more often than not submitting one at all — but a weak/generic one can actively hurt an application, so only send a tailored one.
- Resume still matters more than the cover letter — don't let cover letter polish come at the expense of resume quality.
