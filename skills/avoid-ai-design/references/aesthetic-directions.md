# Aesthetic Directions

A working set of named, committable directions for step 3 of the workflow. Each one names 3–5
defining moves — enough to make every downstream decision (type, color, layout, motion)
answerable by "does this serve the direction?" rather than by taste in the moment. Propose one
of these (or a clear variant) rather than a vague instruction like "make it look more premium."

Pick a direction that fits the *content*, not the one that looks best in isolation — a fintech
dashboard and a children's education app should not converge on the same direction just because
it's currently in fashion.

---

**Swiss / editorial.** Strict grid, generous negative space, one serif or grotesque display face
paired with a plain body sans, a single ink color plus one sharp accent (never a gradient),
asymmetric layout, minimal or no decorative motion. Fits: professional tools, publications,
anything that benefits from feeling authoritative and calm.

**Brutalist editorial.** Raw, high-contrast, deliberately unpolished — visible grid lines or
borders, monospace or oversized display type, flat color blocks instead of gradients or shadows,
abrupt section breaks instead of smooth transitions. Fits: developer tools, portfolios, anything
that wants to signal "no marketing gloss."

**Warm editorial / pastel.** Soft, desaturated palette (not the punchy SaaS-purple family), a
humanist serif or rounded sans, generous rounded imagery, gentle motion (fades, soft parallax).
Fits: consumer wellness, education, community products — anything that wants to feel approachable
without defaulting to purple gradients.

**Refined luxury.** Dark or near-black dominant background, a single metallic or jewel-tone
accent used sparingly, a high-contrast serif display face, tight tracking, restrained motion (one
deliberate reveal, nothing playful). Fits: premium/B2B products, anything selling exclusivity.

**Technical / data-dense.** Monospace or grotesque throughout, a dark or high-contrast theme built
for long sessions, dense information layout with real spatial hierarchy (not uniform spacing),
color reserved strictly for status/data meaning (never decorative). Fits: dashboards, developer
consoles, anything where the UI's job is legibility under density, not first impressions.

**Maximalist / expressive.** Bold, saturated dominant color (not a gradient — a real color
choice), an unconventional type pairing (a distinctive display face, not a "safe" grotesque),
overlapping/diagonal layout elements, one well-orchestrated load animation. Fits: consumer brands,
creative tools, anything that wants to feel alive rather than restrained.

---

## Extracting a direction from a reference

When the person points at a design they like ("make it feel like Linear" / "like this
screenshot"), don't clone it pixel-for-pixel — extract three things and apply them to *this*
product's own content and structure:

1. **Macrostructure** — the actual layout shape (not the specific copy or imagery): where the
   asymmetry is, how sections break, how dense or sparse it is.
2. **Type pairing** — which two faces, and the relationship between them (contrast in weight,
   contrast in style, or a display/body split).
3. **Color anchor** — the one dominant color and how the accent is used, not the exact hex values.

State the extracted direction back to the person in the same three-to-five-move format as the
named directions above, so it's just as committable and auditable against the catalog.
