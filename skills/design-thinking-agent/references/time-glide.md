# The Time Glide

Use before any futurecasting. It makes a forecast specific rather than vague.

## Input

The product or service, and the client's project (so the final step can locate it).

## Procedure

1. **Describe the product/service at four moments:** 10 years ago, 5 years ago, 2 years ago, today.
2. **Use the same seven dimensions at every moment:**
   - Materials (or technology stack, for digital/services)
   - Safety or regulatory standards
   - Who used it
   - What it cost
   - Where it was bought (channel)
   - What happened when it broke (repair/support)
   - How it was disposed of (end of life)
3. **Identify** which dimensions changed fastest and which have not moved at all. The static ones are often as important — they're either stable constraints or overdue for disruption.
4. **Project each fast-moving dimension 2–5 years out.** For each projection, state **what would have to be true** for it to happen (the signposts to watch).
5. **Locate the client's project**: which of those futures is it sitting inside, and what does that mean for what we design now?

If web search is available, ground historical claims (standards, prices, market shifts) in real sources and cite them. Mark estimates as estimates.

## Quality checks

- Same dimensions at every moment (no switching lenses mid-table).
- Actual years shown, computed from today's date.
- Every projection has a "what would have to be true."
- The final step makes a concrete design recommendation, not a generic "stay flexible."
