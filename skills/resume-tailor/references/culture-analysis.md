# Reading company culture & work style from the JD and web

Do this analysis before selecting bullets, not after drafting. It decides what gets
cut, not just how things are worded.

## Step 1 — Mine the JD itself
Read for these signals directly in the posting:
- **Structure words**: "waterfall," "stage-gate," "phase," "milestone-driven,"
  "compliance," "regulated," "documentation-heavy," "hardware," "manufacturing" →
  lean toward structured/sequential-process signals (see Step 3, Waterfall profile).
- **Structure words (other end)**: "matrix," "cross-functional," "squad," "pod,"
  "agile," "sprint," "ambiguity," "0-to-1," "wear many hats," "fast-paced,"
  "scrappy," "startup" → lean toward Matrix/cross-functional profile.
- **Named tools**: JIRA + Confluence + sprint cadence → Agile/matrix. MS Project,
  Gantt, PMP, Six Sigma, ISO → waterfall/process-discipline.
- **Team language**: "own the roadmap end-to-end" (matrix, PM-owns-everything) vs.
  "coordinate across [list of functions]" (matrix, but more facilitator-flavored)
  vs. "execute against the project plan" (waterfall).

## Step 2 — Scrape 2–3 outside signals (use web_search / web_fetch)
- Company careers page / "life at [company]" page — look for words like "agile,"
  "cross-functional pods," "flat," vs. "process," "quality system," "phase-gate."
- Glassdoor/LinkedIn "About" or recent Glassdoor culture reviews (skim, don't
  over-trust one review) — search `"[company]" glassdoor culture agile OR waterfall`.
- Recent press or engineering/product blog posts — do they talk about how they
  ship? (e.g. "we run two-week sprints" vs. "our PMP-certified project managers.")
- Industry baseline as a prior, then confirm/override with the above: consumer
  tech/software startups skew matrix/agile; industrial, aerospace, pharma,
  construction, and large regulated enterprises skew waterfall/stage-gate —
  but always confirm, don't assume from industry alone.

## Step 3 — Two emphasis profiles (a spectrum, not a binary)
Most real companies sit somewhere in between — treat this as a dial, not a switch.

**Waterfall / stage-gate signal → emphasize:**
- Structured, sequential, milestone-driven bullets: PRD writing, phased rollouts,
  documentation, requirements gathering → feasibility review → sign-off →
  execution (the SCOOP feasibility-review bullet is a strong fit here).
- Process rigor: SOPs, work breakdown structures, Lean Six Sigma/DMAIC framing,
  compliance/patent-filing acceleration, quality/consistency metrics.
- Fewer, deeper bullets on 1-2 roles rather than breadth across many teams.

**Matrix / cross-functional signal → emphasize:**
- "Directed a 12-person cross-functional engineering team," "collaborated with
  cross-functional teams," "stakeholder alignment across [functions]."
- Speed and iteration: Agile/Scrum, sprint planning, MVP iteration, A/B testing,
  rapid customer discovery loops.
- Breadth: show activity across more roles/projects rather than depth on one.

**Always true regardless of profile — curiosity & activeness:**
Every resume should read as someone who initiates rather than waits to be told.
Favor bullets/verbs like "identified," "diagnosed," "audited," "prototyped,"
"tested," "closed [a gap]," "built [something from scratch]" over passive
"responsible for" or "assisted with" framing. The Auster/SCOOP bullets ("closed
critical infrastructure gaps... by auditing," "diagnosing and resolving a
stability failure invisible at lower loads") are the house style for this —
they show Samin finding a problem nobody assigned him, not just executing a task.

## Step 4 — Write down the call before drafting
State it in one line before touching the resume, e.g.:
"This is a matrix-leaning consumer-software PM role → lead with Edu-Explorer +
SuperDataScience, keep Auster to 2 bullets, drop Lean Six Sigma coursework line,
keep BRAC leadership bullet for breadth-of-impact signal."
This one-liner is also useful to show Samin so he can sanity-check the read before
you spend time on a full draft.
